An Analysis of Comments on EPA's February Endangerment Rescission

Every comment on EPA's 2025 proposal to rescind the 2009 Greenhouse Gas Endangerment Finding, classified — and every substantive argument checked against what EPA said in response, in the vehicle rule and in the September 2026 power-plant proposal.

Docket · comments · built

Who commented, and what EPA did with it

EPA's September 2026 power-plant proposal describes this docket as "hundreds of thousands of comments from a variety of perspectives" (91 FR 59016), and the vehicle final rule it builds on reported "supportive and adverse comments on virtually all substantive aspects of the proposal from a wide variety of stakeholders" (91 FR 7695). The record shows that more than 98% of comments with a determinable stance opposed the rescission.

EPA counted the comments — "approximately 572,000 written comments from more than 31,000 unique entities and 169 mass letter writing campaigns" (91 FR 7693) — without ever discussing the relative number of comments that supported the rule relative to the ones that opposed it. The vehicle rule names the stakeholder categories it heard from: "vehicle and engine manufacturers and suppliers, nearly all 50 States and the District of Columbia, elected representatives at the local, State, and Federal levels …, consumer and labor groups, EV advocates, manufacturers, and suppliers, educational institutions, environmental groups, and individual citizens." Every one of those categories ran heavily against the rescission except business and industry (28% opposed) and trade associations (33%); the bars below give each one.

The three endangerment arguments, and what commenters said

The three legal arguments underlying the Endangerment rescission in the vehicle and power-plant rules are claims that changes in how the Supreme Court views the major questions doctrine and other issues render the Finding incorrect, that the 1970 Clean Air Act was not intended to address global pollutants, and that the impacts are de minimis.
